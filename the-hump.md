His legal name was Mark Anders. Nobody uses legal names though. Mark “The Hump” Anders was one of the world’s largest individual investors, sporting a portfolio that was the equivalent of Wall Street’s wet dreams. His crown jewel however, lay in one particular stake of ownership: his 51% ownership of Extraordinary Vigilant Intelligence Limited—better known as EVIL.

Mark started EVIL from the ground up—a real rags to riches story with nothing more than a small plot of land in little known San Francisco, a dream of making it big, and the backing of his Daddy’s Fortune 500 holdings company, nothing could stop him. Mr. Anders clawed his way up Wall Street, knocking down his opponents as they came. *Ha! I could buy your company and turn HQ into my pool room. Wouldn’t expect you to get that reference…*

The Hump had climbed Wall Street, and nothing could knock him from the top. Everyday, he walked into his office and placed himself in his chair, like it was the throne to his kingdom. He resided on the 35th floor and often took his time to look down upon the peasants who he could see walking, driving, going along to their little places to eventually, little by little, fulfill their little lives. Him? He was meant for big things. The size of the man represented what he could do to companies: swallow them whole.

The Hump had everything he could ever wish for. What more could a man want? There was however one thorn that pricked him; John King was another one of the world’s largest individual investors, having a portfolio that differed from the Hump’s in the matter of a few million—chump change. King also had a 30% ownership stake in EVIL, just enough to prove a nuisance for certain decisions that required a supermajority for approval.

“Shareholders’ meetings tonight at 8 right?” he screeched over at his secretary.

“Yes it is sir,” she replied, lacking any emotion in her voice. Twenty years of working the same dead-end job did that to a woman.

He smiled in that piggish manner he did, parting the folds of fat on his face that allegedly formed a mouth. Today wasn’t like any other day. He had long schemed with the intent of finding out how to oust his rival from the company, and today? He had struck gold. Hell, this was such a surefire catch, what need was there to run this by the lawyer? The Hump was a seasoned corporate king and he didn’t need his advisors to do all his work for him.

7:50 PM. A group of 15 men—of various sizes—marched into the EVIL buildings. The power seemed to reside equally amongst them. It seemed. 

8:00 PM. The Hump sat across from King, giving him a nod in mutual respect. It was returned. “Let’s begin our meeting for the fifth of September by going over our agenda.” The Hump rattled off agenda item after agenda item, mostly filled with corporate jargon before he dropped a bomb.

“Finally, we the shareholders shall be put to a vote as to whether or not Mr. King’s shares are to be repossessed regarding the discovery of violations, outlined in Article III, Section 5 of our shareholders’ agreement.”

With any of the Hump’s opponents, the emotion would drain out of their face. They would do whatever they could to remain emotionless, a fruitless endeavor, with everything coming to a head when the spotlight eventually faced their way.

The Hump looked at Mr. King’s face expecting to see a similar performance for him to watch. I hope this one cries. Strangely, Mr. King kept the same neutral smile he had upon entering. That’s weird. The Hump didn’t care. He was sure he had him.

Agenda item after agenda item got marked off the list, until finally, it was time. 9:08 PM. The Hump grabbed a folder out of his briefcase and handed each of the fifteen men a copy of a paper, before snatching one for himself. 

“As per Article III, Section V of our shareholders’ agreement, one is not permitted to advise, work with, or otherwise associate alongside rival firms, as Mr. King has clearly violated, due to his voting shares present in Sustainable Trajectory Development,” he paused, shooting a look into Mr. King’s eyes before he continued, “Mr. King, is there anything you have to say?”

Mr. King returned his look, smiling—no laughing almost at his request. 

“Thank you Mr. Anders. I would apologize for an action like this. Believe me, I would. And I’d be happy to accept a punishment in any other circumstance.” he stated, looking firmly at him.

“I’m sorry, Mr. King?” Mr. Anders shot at him.

“You’re excused Mr. Anders. I would just like to point out that your inclusion of a vote of any kind at this meeting, without any due diligence to inform us shareholders, is a violation of the Ethics Code, clearly established in Article VIII, Section II.”

“That doesn’t mean anything, I’ll just hold the vote next—”

“I was just getting there Mr. Anders. Now if you’ll let me finish, Article VIII, Section II also clearly states that ‘a vote containing the possibility of a shareholder being ousted, that is not adequately communicated to all shareholders, shall be interpreted as an act of malice’ which as stated in the preceding section, ‘shall result in a forced buyback of the violator’s shares for $0.01 per share.’”

There was a silence following the statement. The idea of buying back Mr. Anders’ shares echoed in their heads. Him of all people? Did Hell freeze over? Mark’s power was shattered. All the shareholders and all the execs, couldn’t put The Hump together again. The very bylaws Mark drafted—specifically with traps meant to oust others—came back to bite him.

9:15 PM. The meeting was over. Mark exited the meeting room. He had been given a week to pack his things and never show his fat face in the building again. His crown jewel had just been seized from him, yet even without the weight of his crown, he still couldn’t walk with his head held high. His pitiful remaining stock hardly gave him the ability to change a roll of toilet paper, let alone vote at  meetings. Even his precious office and secretary were gone as losing his shares also meant a removal from the company board; he could kiss the C-suite goodbye as well.

Now it was just Mark Anders. Even a prefix before his name was a generous ask. Mark Anders. The Hump was dead.
